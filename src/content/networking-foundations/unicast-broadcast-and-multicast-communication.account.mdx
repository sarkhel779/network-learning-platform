<h2 id="diagnose-delivery-scope">Diagnose delivery-scope scenarios</h2>

Use evidence in order: classify the destination, identify ingress, check learned MAC or receiver-group state, trace eligible egress ports, then separate receipt from acceptance.

### Unexpected flooding

A missing or aged MAC entry can temporarily flood a unicast frame; it does not turn the destination into broadcast.

### Broadcast and relay boundaries

An ordinary Layer 2 broadcast stops at a router. For DHCP, verify whether a configured relay received the local request and generated a separate forwarded request toward the server.

### Multicast symptoms

An unsubscribed host may capture multicast when the switch lacks receiver-group state, then reject it. For another network, verify that multicast routing is explicitly enabled; addressing alone does not create routed distribution.

<h2 id="packet-evidence">Packet evidence</h2>

Capture at more than one observation point when possible. Record destination addressing, ingress and egress links, interface receipt, and host processing. A capture proves receipt at that point—not application acceptance.
