<h2 id="what-is-a-switch">What is a switch?</h2>

A **switch** connects devices in a local network. It is the modern, multi-port development of the bridge idea: instead of treating all connected devices as one shared signal space, it can be selective about which port carries local traffic.

<h2 id="switch-ports">Switch ports</h2>

Each physical switch port is a connection point for a host or another network device. Port labels help people trace cables and understand which devices are connected where.

At this stage, remember the simple relationship: local devices connect to labelled switch ports. The detailed process a switch uses is taught later.

<h2 id="more-selective-than-a-hub">More selective than a hub</h2>

A hub repeats an incoming signal to all other ports. A switch can send local traffic through a suitable port rather than treating every port the same. This makes switches a better fit for modern local networks.

<h2 id="switches-and-routers">Switches and routers</h2>

A switch primarily connects devices inside a local network. A router connects different IP networks. Home equipment may combine both functions in one box, but the roles remain different.
