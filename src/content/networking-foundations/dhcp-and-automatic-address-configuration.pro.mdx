<h2 id="pro-deep-dive">Pro deep dive</h2>

**DHCP snooping** establishes trusted server-facing ports, rejects rogue-server replies on untrusted ports, and can build address bindings used by related protections. Its trust boundary must match the real relay and uplink topology.

**Option 82** carries Relay Agent Information such as Circuit ID and Remote ID. Trace insertion by a trusted relay, echo by the server, and removal before client delivery. Treat untrusted Option 82 input as a policy and spoofing risk.

Model **reservations**, **exclusions**, policy options, and **multiple-server** behavior explicitly. Multiple offers are valid, but scope ownership and reconciliation must prevent conflicting allocations. Advanced capture analysis should correlate `xid`, `secs`, flags, identifiers, requested addresses, option lengths/order, lease timers, server logs, binding state, and relay transformations.

DHCP authentication exists but depends on deployment and key management; operational defenses still commonly include DHCP snooping, port trust, rate limits, and monitoring. **DHCPv4 high availability and failover are vendor-specific or implementation-specific approaches**, not an Internet-standard failover protocol established by the RFC below.

In the IPv6 boundary, DHCPv6 can be stateful, stateless, or coexist with SLAAC; it also supports **prefix delegation**. **RFC 8156 defines DHCPv6 failover**, not DHCPv4 failover.

Primary references: [RFC 2131](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2131), [RFC 2132](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2132), [RFC 3046](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3046), [RFC 3118](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3118), [RFC 3442](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3442), [RFC 6607](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6607), and [RFC 8156](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8156).
