## Why delivery scope matters {#why-delivery-scope-matters}

Delivery scope answers a practical question: which interfaces receive a transmission, and which hosts actually accept and process it?

## Unicast: one intended destination {#unicast-one-destination}

Unicast identifies one intended destination. A switch may forward an unknown unicast through several ports while it learns, but the destination remains unicast.

## Broadcast: the local broadcast domain {#broadcast-local-domain}

A broadcast is delivered throughout its local broadcast domain. A router can receive it on a local interface, but does not ordinarily forward it into another network.

## Multicast: an interested receiver group {#multicast-receiver-group}

Multicast addresses a receiver group. Hosts accept the traffic only when they have joined that group, while efficient forwarding depends on group-aware network state.

## Unknown unicast is not broadcast {#unknown-unicast-is-not-broadcast}

Flooding describes a switch action. It does not rewrite the original unicast destination into a broadcast address.
