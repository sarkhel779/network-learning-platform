This lesson distinguishes the hosts that create and receive traffic from the network devices that connect them.

<h2 id="what-is-a-host">What is a host?</h2>

A host is an end device that originates, receives, or processes network data.

<h2 id="connecting-devices">Devices that connect hosts</h2>

Switches, access points, routers, and firewalls connect hosts and guide their traffic in different ways.

<h2 id="explore-topology">Explore the topology</h2>

The interactive device explorer will connect each device to its role in the topology.

<h2 id="compare-journeys">Compare packet journeys</h2>

The packet comparisons will show wired and wireless hosts reaching local and remote servers.

<h2 id="windows-checks">Windows checks</h2>

Windows commands provide evidence about the host, its local network, and its default gateway.

<h2 id="wireshark-checks">Wireshark checks</h2>

Basic display filters help connect captured frames and packets to the active network path.

<h2 id="test-understanding">Test your understanding</h2>

Short questions will test device classification and local-versus-remote decisions.

<h2 id="summary">Summary</h2>

Hosts create or receive traffic; intermediary devices connect hosts and forward that traffic.
