<h2 id="why-vlans-exist">Why VLANs exist</h2>

VLANs divide one physical switching environment into separate logical Layer 2 broadcast domains.

<h2 id="access-ports-and-membership">Access ports and membership</h2>

An access port assigns ordinary endpoint traffic to one operational VLAN.

<h2 id="interactive-vlan-membership">Interactive VLAN membership</h2>

Choose a broadcast or unicast journey. The switch classifies the incoming frame by its access-port membership, then exposes every eligible egress interface.

Without JavaScript, remember the same rule: a frame entering an access port joins that port's VLAN. A broadcast is copied only to other eligible ports in that VLAN, never back through the ingress port.

<h2 id="trunks-and-802-1q">Trunks and 802.1Q</h2>

A trunk preserves VLAN identity while carrying multiple VLANs between network devices.

<h2 id="interactive-tag-journey">Interactive tag journey</h2>

The second player will follow a frame as its VLAN tag is added and removed.

<h2 id="allowed-vlans-and-routing-boundary">Allowed VLANs and routing boundary</h2>

Different VLANs require Layer 3 routing to communicate.
