<h2 id="what-is-a-computer">What is a computer?</h2>

A **computer** is an electronic device that follows instructions to process data. **Data** is information it can work with: numbers, words, pictures or sounds.

You do not need to understand everything inside a computer to start learning networking. First, let's see how one computer turns a simple request into a result.

<h2 id="how-a-computer-works">How does a computer work?</h2>

Suppose you use a calculator app to add **2 + 2**. Follow the three steps below:

<figure className="computer-basics-flow" aria-label="Input, processing and output, with storage connected to processing">
  <figcaption>One computer: from your request to a result</figcaption>

The **CPU (central processing unit)** executes instructions. **RAM** is temporary working memory. **Storage**, such as an SSD, keeps applications and saved files. For example, you can save a document to open again later.

**Now connect the idea to networking:** your computer can create and save a document by itself. But what if you want another device to receive it? That is where a network becomes useful.

<h2 id="what-is-a-computer-network">What is a computer network?</h2>

Imagine you want to print a document from your laptop. The printer is across the room, with no cable directly connecting it to your laptop. How can your document reach it?

Both devices are connected to the same network. In this simple wired example, a switch connects them. Select each device below to meet its role.

**A network lets connected devices exchange data and share resources.** That connected group of devices is a **computer network**. The connections can be wired or wireless.

<h2 id="lan-wan-and-the-internet">LANs, WANs and the Internet</h2>

A **local area network (LAN)** connects devices in a limited place such as a home, office, or school. A **wide area network (WAN)** connects networks across larger distances. The **Internet** is a global system of interconnected networks.
