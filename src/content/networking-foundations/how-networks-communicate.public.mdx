<h2 id="what-is-a-computer-network">What is a computer network?</h2>

A **computer network** is a group of connected devices that can exchange data and share resources. The devices may be close together in one room or separated across countries. What makes them a network is their ability to communicate through agreed rules and connections.

**A network lets connected devices exchange data and share resources.** For example, a laptop can send a document to a printer, or a phone can request a page from a web server.

<h2 id="why-networks-exist">Why networks exist</h2>

Networks make it practical to share information, devices, and services. A family can share one Internet connection. An office can share printers and files. A school can make the same learning service available to many students.

The useful result is communication: one device creates or requests information and another device receives or supplies it.

<h2 id="lan-wan-and-the-internet">LANs, WANs and the Internet</h2>

A **local area network (LAN)** connects devices in a limited place such as a home, office, or school. A **wide area network (WAN)** connects networks across larger distances. The **Internet** is a global system of interconnected networks.

These names describe scope. They do not change the basic idea: connected devices exchange data through network links and intermediary devices.

<h2 id="end-and-intermediary-devices">End and intermediary devices</h2>

An **end device**, or host, is where user data is created or received. Computers, phones, printers, cameras, and servers can be hosts. An **intermediary device** helps connect, direct, or protect traffic. Switches, routers, wireless access points, and firewalls are common examples.
