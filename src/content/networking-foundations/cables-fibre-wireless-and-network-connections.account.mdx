<h2 id="design-a-connection">Design a connection</h2>

Choose a medium by comparing the requirement with distance, environment, capacity, reliability,
mobility, compatibility, installation, and cost. Campus buildings need a connection chosen for
distance and environmental exposure. A nearby desktop is a fixed, short connection to a local
router. The recommended connection for a nearby desktop is copper Ethernet.

<h2 id="diagnose-link-symptoms">Diagnose link symptoms</h2>

Start with the symptom and scope, then check power, connection, and link state. Compare
negotiated speed and duplex where applicable; inspect distance, cable condition, seating, signal
strength, and interference; change one variable; then retest and record the evidence. A lit link
indicator proves physical or link negotiation, not end-to-end connectivity.

<h2 id="knowledge-check-summary">Knowledge check and summary</h2>

Use evidence to separate a damaged cable, excessive distance, duplex mismatch, weak signal, and
radio interference. Low throughput alone does not prove a bad cable, and weak wireless does not
identify the exact interference source.
